Mandatory requirements checklist
Source: Solar_Installation_Management_System.pdf, “A Week at SunPower” and “Packages”.
Check a box when the behaviour works end to end.

1. Enquiries and site approval
[ ] Keep a sales record for enquiries received through WhatsApp, calls, and the web form. Web submissions must reach sales immediately with the customer's name, phone, address, and needs, including a subsidy request when applicable.
[ ] Record the qualification call covering shadow-free roof area, monthly electricity bill, and grid suitability.
[ ] For a suitable site, record the technician's survey findings on roof direction, tilt, and cable distance.
[ ] Allow a quote to be sent to the customer only after survey approval.

2. Packages, quotations, and confirmed orders
[ ] Offer a solar panel hardware set with panels, connectors, and wires, plus an installation service covering on-site fitting and testing.
[ ] Support on-grid and off-grid systems from 1 kW to 5 kW. The on-grid kit includes panels, inverter, wiring boxes, charge controller, safety earthing, and metal stands. The off-grid kit adds a storage battery.
[ ] Generate quotes from ready-made templates. The selected package and capacity must supply the complete parts list, including switches, wiring, and safety parts.
[ ] Record an advance or full payment before work starts when paid. The brief describes this as the usual payment flow.
[ ] On order confirmation, automatically give the warehouse its packing list and put the installation job on the crew's schedule.

3. Dispatch, delivery, and warranties
[ ] Track stock for high-value equipment such as panels, inverters, and batteries.
[ ] Require a serial scan for every inverter, battery, and charge controller before it leaves the warehouse. Link each dispatched unit to its customer.
[ ] On completed delivery, automatically start the warranty for each serial number using the applicable product term. Example terms in the brief are 2 years for an inverter or battery and 20 years for a panel.

4. Installation and customer acceptance
[ ] Support the crew's field workflow through mounting stands, laying panels, connecting boxes, completing earthing, and testing power generation.
[ ] Provide a phone- or tablet-friendly completion form for start time, finish time, installed system size, and finished-roof photos.
[ ] Capture the customer's on-screen acceptance signature and retain it with the installation record as proof of work.

5. Breakdowns and after-sales service
[ ] Open a complaint ticket and quickly retrieve the customer's installation, exact equipment serial number, and warranty status.
[ ] Provide an in-warranty repair or replacement at no charge. Automatically bill parts and labour for an out-of-warranty repair.
[ ] Track the ticket until the customer confirms the system works.

6. Finance and management reporting
[ ] Track advances, send invoices, record stock purchases, capture technician hours, and track technician pay.
[ ] Track government subsidy paperwork and outstanding steps for each customer.
[ ] Show revenue, costs, and profit or loss for each installation so the director can identify profitable and loss-making jobs.
[ ] Provide daily, weekly, and monthly views of performance, unfinished installations, and open repairs. Allow the director to share the figures.

# Photonix — Additional Project Requirements

The official Solar Installation Management System problem statement has already been provided. All its mandatory requirements must remain intact.

The following additional requirements address real-world challenges faced by Indian rooftop solar customers and installation companies.

The primary objective is to make solar adoption more affordable, accessible, transparent and reliable for customers while improving revenue, profitability and operational efficiency for companies.

## 1.Photonix — Accessible Customer Acquisition

Problem: Potential customers lack awareness of solar technology, subsidies, financing options and installation procedures. Customers with limited digital literacy struggle to initiate the process.

Requirements:

- Simplified customer onboarding without requiring technical knowledge.
- English and Hindi language support.
- Employee-assisted registration for customers enquiring through telephone calls or physical visits.
- Collect customer requirements, including:
  - Residential or commercial property.
  - Monthly electricity consumption and bill.
  - Available rooftop space.
  - Installation budget.
  - Power backup requirements.
  - Preferred payment method.
- Recommend a preliminary solar system capacity based on customer requirements.
- Explain on-grid and off-grid systems in understandable language.
- Display relevant benefits, limitations and estimated costs.
- Provide preliminary government subsidy eligibility information.
- Explain available financing and installment options without guaranteeing loan approval.
- Automatically register customer enquiries in the CRM.
- Track individual customer requirements, concerns, communication history and follow-ups.
- Identify unattended enquiries and notify responsible employees.
- Require technical site survey approval before generating the final quotation.

Expected business impact: Increased customer acquisition, improved conversion rates, reduced lost leads and accessibility for non-technical customers.

Metrics:
- Lead conversion rate.
- Missed enquiries.
- Customer onboarding time.
- Average lead response time.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Enquiry-to-installation conversion rate.

## 2. SolarFit — Intelligent Affordability and Profit Protection

Priority: Primary product differentiator.

Problem: Customers face expensive installations, unclear pricing and unsuitable system recommendations. Companies face fluctuating equipment prices, procurement expenses and declining profit margins.

Requirements:

- Recommend suitable solar packages between 1 kW and 5 kW based on customer needs.
- Prevent unnecessary system oversizing.
- Generate multiple suitable package options, such as economical, balanced and premium.
- Compare packages based on:
  - Installation cost.
  - Equipment specifications.
  - Estimated power generation.
  - Warranty coverage.
  - Expected electricity savings.
  - Estimated payback period.
  - Customer budget.
- Calculate estimated installation costs using actual available product and procurement prices.
- Display transparent cost breakdowns, including equipment, labour, transportation and applicable taxes.
- Display gross installation price, estimated subsidy and effective post-subsidy cost separately.
- Distinguish residential and commercial subsidy eligibility.
- Provide indicative EMI and financing cost calculations.
- Calculate estimated monthly and annual electricity savings.
- Calculate simple return on investment and payback period.
- Keep electricity tariffs, generation assumptions and subsidy rules configurable.
- Generate accurate bills of materials from selected packages.
- Identify compatible, lower-cost equipment alternatives without compromising safety, quality or performance.
- Compare supplier prices, stock availability, warranty coverage and delivery timelines.
- Recommend cost-efficient procurement options.
- Alert employees when equipment prices increase after quotation creation.
- Maintain quotation validity periods and historical pricing.
- Calculate expected project contribution before confirming a quotation.
- Prevent or flag quotations below the company's configurable minimum profit margin.
- Identify opportunities to pass a portion of operational savings to customers while maintaining company profitability.
- Compare estimated project costs against actual expenses.
- Identify the reasons behind project cost overruns.
- Clearly communicate that estimated savings, subsidies and payback periods are conditional and not guaranteed.

Expected business impact: Lower customer expenditure, transparent pricing, reduced unnecessary procurement costs, improved sales conversion and protected company margins.

Metrics:
- Average installation cost per kW.
- Customer cost savings.
- Procurement cost variance.
- Quotation acceptance rate.
- Estimated versus actual project cost.
- Contribution margin per installation.
- Number of loss-making projects.
- Amount of preventable cost leakage.

## 3. SubsidyBridge — Simplified Government Documentation

Problem: Customers struggle to understand subsidy procedures, documentation requirements and government approval statuses. Installation companies lose time and customers due to administrative delays.

Requirements:

- Provide a centralized digital case file for every applicable customer.
- Explain the subsidy application process using simple step-by-step guidance.
- Automatically generate applicable document checklists based on customer and project type.
- Allow authorized employees to upload and verify required documents.
- Identify missing, incomplete or rejected documents.
- Track government application reference numbers.
- Track applicable milestones, including:
  - Application registration.
  - Feasibility approval.
  - Documentation verification.
  - Installation completion.
  - DISCOM inspection.
  - Net-meter installation.
  - Commissioning.
  - Subsidy claim.
  - Subsidy disbursement.
- Display current status, pending requirements and next required action.
- Assign responsibility for each pending action.
- Identify delays caused by customers, installation companies, suppliers or government authorities.
- Notify responsible employees about overdue actions and incomplete documentation.
- Provide customers with a simple installation and subsidy progress tracker.
- Maintain a timestamped history of application status changes.
- Provide access to the official government portal.
- Protect sensitive personal and financial documents.
- Use verified manual updates unless an authorized government integration is available.
- Never generate false approval statuses or guarantee government processing timelines.

Expected business impact: Reduced preventable documentation errors, fewer customer follow-up calls, improved transparency and faster completion of internally controllable processes.

Metrics:
- Application processing time.
- Documentation rejection rate.
- Incomplete applications.
- Overdue milestones.
- Average project delay.
- Projects blocked by government dependencies.
- Outstanding payments associated with delayed projects.

## 4. SmartOps — Proactive Installation and Resource Management

Problem: Installation companies experience technician shortages, scheduling conflicts, unavailable materials, repeat visits and unnecessary operational expenditure.

Requirements:

- Implement an installation readiness assessment for every confirmed project.
- Verify the following prerequisites before releasing installation work:
  - Approved site survey.
  - Confirmed customer requirements.
  - Accepted quotation.
  - Required advance payment.
  - Complete material availability.
  - Equipment compatibility.
  - Applicable government approvals.
  - Qualified technician availability.
  - Site and safety readiness.
- Display an installation readiness score and individual blocking reasons.
- Prevent installation release when mandatory prerequisites remain incomplete.
- Identify missing materials before dispatch.
- Recommend suitable technicians based on:
  - Technical qualifications.
  - Relevant skills.
  - Availability.
  - Current workload.
  - Customer location.
  - Previous job experience.
- Prevent overlapping technician assignments.
- Group nearby installation and maintenance jobs where practical to reduce transportation costs.
- Track estimated and actual installation duration.
- Provide technicians with simplified access to assigned jobs and customer site information.
- Require relevant installation quality and safety checklists.
- Identify damaged, missing or incompatible equipment.
- Record reasons for installation delays and cancellations.
- Track repeat visits, corrective work and associated expenses.
- Automatically associate labour, transportation and additional material expenses with the relevant project.
- Identify technicians or teams requiring additional training based on recurring quality issues.
- Maintain complete installation evidence and customer acceptance records.

Expected business impact: Fewer installation delays, reduced unnecessary visits, improved workforce utilization, lower operational expenses and increased installation capacity.

Metrics:
- Technician utilization rate.
- Technician idle hours.
- Installations completed per month.
- Average installation turnaround time.
- Transportation cost per installation.
- First-time installation success rate.
- Repeat visit rate.
- Installation cost overruns.
- Number of blocked projects.

## 5. SolarCare — Lifetime Customer Support and Trust

Problem: Customers experience poor after-sales support, missing warranty information, unexpected repair charges and difficulty identifying responsible service providers.

Requirements:

- Provide a digital solar equipment passport for every installed system.
- Include equipment information, serial numbers, installation details, warranty conditions and service history.
- Enable QR-based identification of installed systems.
- Allow employees to retrieve customer equipment records using registered customer details or serial numbers.
- Allow customers to report equipment issues through a simplified interface.
- Support employee-assisted complaint registration for customers with limited digital literacy.
- Automatically retrieve applicable warranty information when a service request is created.
- Determine potential warranty coverage and distinguish covered and non-covered repairs.
- Provide transparent repair estimates before performing chargeable work.
- Prioritize service requests based on severity and customer impact.
- Track technician assignments and service resolution progress.
- Notify customers about relevant service updates.
- Maintain complete repair and equipment replacement histories.
- Schedule preventive maintenance based on applicable maintenance requirements.
- Notify responsible employees about approaching warranty expirations.
- Offer paid maintenance plans where services are not already covered.
- Collect customer satisfaction feedback after installation and service resolution.
- Identify recurring equipment failures and associated suppliers.
- Enable customer referrals following satisfactory installation or service experiences.

Expected business impact: Improved customer trust, reduced service administration costs, customer retention, referral-based acquisition and recurring maintenance revenue opportunities.

Metrics:
- Average complaint response time.
- Average service resolution time.
- First-visit resolution rate.
- Warranty claim recovery.
- Cost per service request.
- Repeat equipment failure rate.
- Customer satisfaction score.
- Customer referral rate.
- Maintenance service revenue.

## 6. Financial Risk and Business Growth Intelligence

Problem: Companies cannot accurately identify financial losses, delayed payments, unprofitable installations or operational bottlenecks.

Requirements:

- Provide project-wise financial visibility.
- Track estimated and actual revenue, direct costs and contribution margins.
- Track material, labour, transportation, rework and maintenance expenditure.
- Identify projects exceeding their approved budgets.
- Identify loss-making and low-margin projects.
- Track customer advances, pending payments and overdue invoices.
- Track upcoming supplier payments and outstanding receivables.
- Calculate working capital requirements for upcoming installations.
- Identify projects where insufficient cash or inventory may delay execution.
- Provide actionable business alerts, including:
  - Unattended customer enquiries.
  - Low-margin quotations.
  - Increasing procurement costs.
  - Insufficient inventory.
  - Overdue customer payments.
  - Incomplete documentation.
  - Installation readiness failures.
  - Technician scheduling conflicts.
  - Overdue service requests.
  - Unexpected project expenses.
- Quantify the estimated financial impact of identifiable operational problems.
- Display recommended corrective actions based on actual business data.
- Distinguish revenue, cash received, contribution margin and net profit.
- Provide daily, weekly and monthly business performance summaries.
- Track improvements against historical business performance.
- Never present fabricated savings, financial improvements or business metrics as actual results.

Expected business impact: Better financial decisions, reduced revenue leakage, improved cash flow, stronger profitability and sustainable business expansion.

## 7. Product Accessibility and Usability Requirements

- Design customer-facing interfaces for people without technical knowledge.
- Minimize the number of steps required to submit enquiries and service requests.
- Use understandable terminology instead of unnecessary technical jargon.
- Provide mobile-friendly customer and technician interfaces.
- Support both self-service and employee-assisted workflows.
- Allow customers to track their individual installation journey.
- Avoid requiring customers to repeatedly provide information already available in the system.
- Maintain transparent pricing, service charges and project statuses.
- Protect customer documents and personal information.
- Ensure different employees access only information relevant to their responsibilities.

## 8. Integration Requirements

- All additional features must operate within the original Solar Installation Management System.
- Maintain one centralized customer and project history throughout the entire lifecycle.
- Customer recommendations must connect to CRM and quotations.
- Pricing calculations must use product, inventory and procurement information.
- Financial information must reflect actual sales, payments and operational expenses.
- Documentation progress must influence installation readiness where applicable.
- Technician activities must update project progress and actual labour costs.
- Equipment dispatch and delivery must connect to customer asset and warranty records.
- Service requests must retrieve the corresponding customer, equipment and warranty information.
- Business metrics must be derived from actual recorded transactions and workflow events.
- Avoid disconnected features, duplicate data entry and redundant business processes.
